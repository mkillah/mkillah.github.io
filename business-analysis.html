I find it more useful to show how I work than to list skills. This case study walks through a complete business analysis engagement, from a one-page problem statement to an MVP running in production, with every deliverable and what it concretely produced.

Phase 1

Discovery & alignment

Understand the problem before touching the solution.

01Week 1

Problem statement

A one-page brief defining who is affected, what the problem is, and why it matters.

“NordPay loses €22K/month because ops staff reconcile transactions across 4 disconnected sources, creating settlement conflicts with no single source of truth.”

Outcome Approved by the CFO and CTO in Week 1, and used as the scope north star for the whole engagement.

03Weeks 1–2

Stakeholder interviews

Six interviews, some of them iterative, to map each stakeholder's needs and influence on the project.

  • Ops manager
  • 2 reconciliation analysts
  • Risk & Compliance lead
  • Finance
  • Merchant Support

Key find Merchant Support revealed that merchants called every day to ask about settlement status. This was not in the original brief, and became the Merchant Portal feature.

04Week 2

Domain glossary

26 terms defined and agreed across departments in Week 2.

“transaction”
Risk an authorised event Finance a settled ledger entry Operations a reportable case

Critical find “Transaction” meant three different things to Risk, Finance, and Operations. Resolving it before the first wireframe prevented about 3 weeks of back-and-forth in development.

06Week 3

User journey mapping

Journeys mapped for all three personas across the transaction lifecycle: workflows, touchpoints, pain points, and information needs.

Key find Two touchpoints in the existing process had no digital equivalent. They became requirements for the Fraud Analyst Console and the Merchant Portal before wireframing began.

08Week 3

Wireframes & walkthrough

8 screen flows built in Figma over 3 days, then validated in a 90-minute stakeholder walkthrough so everyone shared the same picture of the solution.

Ops manager persona · real-time view of transactions, settlements, held items, and ledger sync.

Reconciliation analyst persona · business rules enforced live: an amount above 90% of the merchant's fraud threshold is held for review.

Fraud analyst · every held transaction in one place, with the reason it was held and a one-click decision.

Outcome 2 minor label changes and zero functional changes in the walkthrough. Scope locked.

09Week 4

Assumptions & constraints

11 assumptions and constraints documented and tracked.

Most impactful The core banking ledger API is rate-limited to 100 calls per minute. This shaped the entire sync architecture (a 15-minute asynchronous settlement queue), and was caught here rather than in Sprint 3.

10Week 4

Business rules

17 rules captured before the schema was designed, not after something broke. Two examples:

Merchant identity

A merchant cannot exist twice under two internal IDs. Every reference points to one real merchant.

Fraud hold

A transaction above 90% of the merchant's fraud threshold, or with unusual activity, is held for a fraud analyst instead of being approved automatically.

Outcome Agreeing the rules up front prevented 3 architecture debates later in development.

Outcome The “Merchant” entity existed twice, in core banking and in the CRM. It was resolved into one canonical merchant before the database schema was created.

12Week 4

Non-functional requirements

19 requirements covering how the system must behave, not just what it does.

Performance

<2s page load · <500ms API · 60 concurrent users

Availability

99.5% SLA · graceful degradation when the ledger is offline

Security

Role-based access (4 levels) · encryption at rest and in transit · full audit log

Compliance

GDPR · PSD2 strong customer authentication · DORA operational resilience · Danish data residency · PII and PCI fields flagged in the data model

Phase 2

Proof of concept

Translate understanding into requirements and working software.

13Weeks 5–6

Prototype & usability sessions

The wireframes became a working prototype that real analysts could click through, not just review.

  • 9 pages, each traceable to a user goal
  • 38 user actions written as dev specs
  • 5 usability sessions with real analysts

Each user action was specified as trigger → decision → outcome, so developers could build directly from it.

Key find Bulk settlement import was critical for analysts but missing from scope. It was added as a Must-Have and approved within 24 hours.

15Weeks 7–8

Requirements & Definition of Done

What “done” means for each story: testable, unambiguous, and agreed before development starts.

User story

As a reconciliation analyst, I want to create a single transaction with automatic fraud-threshold validation, so that only risky transactions wait for review.

Acceptance criteria · within threshold
Given
an analyst enters the transaction details
When
the amount is within the merchant's fraud threshold
Then
the transaction is created as authorised, with no manual review
Acceptance criteria · above threshold
Given
an analyst enters the transaction details
When
the amount exceeds the merchant's fraud threshold
Then
the transaction is held, a flag is created, and the analyst sees why it was not approved automatically

Edge cases 34 documented. The most impactful: if the ledger times out, transactions wait in a 15-minute local queue with the sync status shown, and no analyst action is needed.

Definition of Done

Ratified by development, QA, and the business

  • Code reviewed and merged
  • Unit tests passing
  • All acceptance criteria verified by QA
  • GDPR and PSD2 reviewed for stories touching PII or card data
  • Performance tested at 60 concurrent users
  • Product owner sign-off in staging

Sign-off Stakeholders signed off in Week 7, followed by zero scope changes in Sprints 1–3.

CONTINUOUS · TEAM MANAGEMENT

Governance without the overhead.

Weekly steering

A 30-minute RAG status for the CFO and CTO. 4 change requests logged: 2 approved, 2 deferred.

RACI matrix

Making Finance Consulted rather than Accountable on the data model eliminated a 3-week back-and-forth.

Change control

Bulk settlement import: a 2-day impact assessed and approved within 24 hours, with zero sprint disruption.
